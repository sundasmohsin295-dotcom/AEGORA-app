## Core Pillars & Architectural Subsystems

### 1. Cyber Twin 5.0 (22 Explainable Dimensions)
1. **Theoretical Knowledge**: Foundational concepts across networking, operating systems, and cryptography.
2. **Practical Ability**: Hands-on sandbox execution speed and command fluency.
3. **Investigation**: Multi-source log correlation and root-cause reconstruction.
4. **Triage**: Rapid discrimination between true positives and benign noise.
5. **Causal Reasoning**: Evidence-first deductive logic across the attack lifecycle.
6. **Decision Making**: Containment precision vs. business downtime trade-off analysis.
7. **Pressure Performance**: Accuracy under active countdown timers and crisis events.
8. **Communication**: Executive risk briefing without unparsed technical jargon.
9. **Cross-Domain Transfer**: Mapping concepts from host systems to cloud and network fabrics.
10. **Retention**: Ebbinghaus spaced retention across core procedural knowledge.
11. **Career Readiness**: Objective alignment with live industry job requirements.
12. **Confidence Calibration**: Metacognitive awareness of actual vs. perceived capability.
13. **Learning Velocity**: Rate of new skill acquisition per active training hour.
14. **Mistake DNA Resistance**: Elimination of recurring cognitive traps.
15. **Evidence Quality**: Cryptographic backing of all claimed competencies.
16. **Independence**: Autonomous problem solving without AI scaffolding.
17. **Consistency**: Daily habit cadence and deliberate practice regularity.
18. **Transferability**: Adapting attack/defense patterns to unfamiliar architectures.
19. **Adaptability**: Dynamic pivot capability when adversary changes TTPs.
20. **Problem Solving**: Deconstructing complex multi-stage vulnerabilities.
21. **Tool Fluency**: Syntax and command-line speed across SIEM, EDR, and CLI tools.
22. **Research Ability**: Threat intelligence synthesis from CVEs and whitepapers.

### 2. Cyber Reality Graph
A linked knowledge and entity graph connecting 18 node types:
- `Career` → `Skill` → `Subskill` → `Tool` → `Framework` → `Technique` → `Attack` → `Defense` → `Lab` → `Project` → `Certification` → `Job` → `Company` → `Incident` → `Threat Actor` → `CVE` → `Research` → `Opportunity`.

### 3. Adaptive Learning Engine & 13 Learning Modes
- **Beginner Discovery**: Zero-jargon mental models and everyday metaphors.
- **Socratic Tutor**: AI guides via diagnostic questions rather than revealing answers.
- **Feynman Arena**: Explain concepts simply back to different audience personas.
- **Retrieval Practice**: Closed-book memory retrieval to strengthen synaptic retention.
- **Investigation Mode**: Raw logs, PCAPs, and Sysmon artifacts with evidence-first triage.
- **Simulation Mode**: Enterprise workplace incident with live branching consequences.
- **Teach-Back Mode**: Train and guide an AI junior analyst on defense methodologies.
- **Pressure Mode**: High-stress active incident containment under tight time clocks.
- **Project Mode**: Build real detection engines, parsers, and defense tooling.
- **Interview Mode**: STAR technical and behavioral questioning with real-time feedback.
- **Research Mode**: Analyze CVE disclosures, threat actor advisories, and crypto papers.
- **Reverse Engineering Mode**: Disassemble binaries, inspect shellcode, and deobfuscate scripts.
- **Executive Mode**: Brief non-technical C-suite leadership on business risks and downtime.
